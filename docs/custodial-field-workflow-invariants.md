# Custodial Field Workflow Invariants

**Status:** Authoritative operational constraint  
**Date:** 2026-07-22  
**Applies to:** current employee kiosk phones, proposed employee Capacitor app, manager views that describe employee work, schedule displays, scan workflow, UX prototypes, and future redesign work

This document preserves the working behavior of the Memphis Zoo Custodial System while its interface is redesigned. It supersedes any conflicting product language that describes an employee having a software-selected **current assignment**, **next assignment**, ordered route, or a primary **Scanner / Scan & Clean page** that must be opened manually.

The design may change. The operating model does not.

## 1. Canonical operating model

The system assigns each custodian a set of areas for the applicable schedule window. Those areas are ownership responsibilities, not a turn-by-turn route generated by the phone.

The custodian:

1. Reviews the currently assigned area list.
2. Chooses a practical cleaning order based on geography, restroom needs, guest conditions, access, and work already underway.
3. Walks to an assigned location.
4. Scans that location's NFC tag.
5. Enters the location-specific Start Cleaning workflow.
6. Completes the work and the correct restroom or exhibit completion form.
7. Submits the record, including issues and services performed.
8. Chooses another assigned area.

The application records ownership, presence, timing, services, exceptions, and completion. It does not micromanage the employee's walking sequence.

## 2. NFC is an ambient entry action, not a destination

### Required normal behavior

- The employee does **not** open a Scanner page before scanning.
- NFC listening is available from the employee kiosk lock screen and throughout the employee app shell.
- Tapping a valid location tag immediately resolves the location and opens its Start Cleaning screen.
- The scanned location is visually confirmed before a cleaning session is started.
- The assigned employee identity comes from the enrolled kiosk/device identity in normal production operation.
- The employee is not repeatedly asked to choose a name when the device is already assigned.
- The scan creates the physical-presence and location context used by the existing scan/session state machine.

### Lock-screen behavior

"Locked" in this workflow means the zoo's employee kiosk/app lock state. While that lock screen is displayed, a valid NFC tag remains a direct operational entry point. The employee should not have to unlock the Hub, open a menu, tap Scanner, and scan again.

### Manual fallback

A manual location lookup or QR/camera fallback may exist for:

- a damaged or missing NFC tag;
- an NFC hardware or permission failure;
- controlled testing or manager-supported recovery.

The fallback is an exception path, not a primary tab. Use of the fallback must be identified in the operational record when technically possible.

## 3. Assigned areas are not an ordered route

Employee language must use:

- **Assigned Areas**
- **Your Areas Today**
- **Areas You Own Now**
- **Current Schedule**

Employee language must not imply a system-generated sequence through labels such as:

- Current Assignment
- Next Assignment
- Next Stop
- Recommended Next Location
- Route Step 1 / Step 2 / Step 3

unless a manager explicitly creates a temporary ordered instruction for an unusual event or emergency.

### Example

If Tammy owns Teton Restrooms, Teton, North West Passage, East End Restrooms, and East End Break Room, the application must not direct her through an inefficient sequence such as:

1. Teton Restrooms
2. East End Restrooms
3. Teton exhibit

She may clean Teton Restrooms and Teton together, then move to another geographic cluster. The phone presents ownership and status; Tammy selects the sensible order.

## 4. Restrooms first means display priority, not forced travel order

Public restrooms are visually listed before exhibits and other areas because they are normally the highest opening priority and need fast reference.

This rule means:

- show assigned public restrooms first;
- show exhibits and other assigned areas after them;
- clearly distinguish restroom and non-restroom work;
- make due-soon, overdue, guest-issue, or changed-ownership states visible.

It does **not** mean the application must send the employee to every restroom across the zoo before cleaning a nearby exhibit.

A custodian may group work geographically. Operational urgency, guest conditions, access, and manager direction may change the order.

## 5. Route coherence belongs to scheduling, not phone sequencing

The scheduler should create coherent area ownership before the employee sees the list. It should:

- favor proximity and practical zoo zones;
- avoid far-east/far-west route stretch unless business needs require it;
- keep related locations together when established operational rules require it;
- preserve stable normal assignments when no absence, CoverAll addition, event, or coverage change requires movement;
- minimize churn and unnecessary walking;
- balance workload without scattering one employee across the property merely to equalize raw counts.

The employee interface must not compensate for a poor assignment by inventing a forced sequence. Assignment quality is corrected at the scheduler/manager level.

## 6. Time-window ownership changes remain supported

The assigned-area list may legitimately change during the day because of:

- the 9:45 restroom rebalance;
- PTO or absence redistribution;
- lunch coverage;
- CoverAll/helper additions;
- varied employee start/end times;
- late or call coverage;
- an event or emergency reassignment;
- a direct manager change.

When ownership changes:

- update the employee's current assigned-area list;
- make added and removed areas clear without duplicating the entire route unnecessarily;
- preserve completed work and active sessions;
- do not reorder unaffected areas into a supposed travel sequence;
- route alerts and guest issues to the employee who owns the location in the current schedule window.

If nothing changes at 9:45, show one clean list rather than artificial morning and afternoon copies.

## 7. Scan and cleaning state must survive interruption

The employee device must preserve an active workflow through screen off, wake, app backgrounding, WebView restoration, and ordinary network interruption.

Preserve at minimum:

- scanned location;
- assigned employee/device identity;
- active cleaning timer;
- started/finished/submission state;
- selected services;
- issue selections and notes;
- offline queue state;
- pending completion submission.

An idle device may return to the employee kiosk lock screen. A device with an active cleaning timer or unfinished completion form returns to that workflow.

## 8. Correct employee app information architecture

The future employee Capacitor edition should preserve the NFC-first model.

Recommended primary destinations:

1. **Assigned Areas** — employee identity, shift information, the current ownership list, restrooms visually first, completed/status indicators, and schedule-window changes.
2. **Messages** — employee conversations, manager messages, Memphis, guest-issue alerts, and operational notices as authorized.
3. **Schedule** — the readable daily ownership view and legitimate coverage changes; not a software-generated walking itinerary.
4. **Events / Notices** — relevant event effects and same-day operational notices.
5. **Report / More** — maintenance, supplies, guest cleanliness follow-up, feedback, help, and the logged manual scan fallback.

There is no required Scanner tab. NFC is handled by the app shell from the lock screen and other employee views. A successful tag scan temporarily takes over the interface with the resolved location workflow, then returns the employee to the appropriate prior context or Assigned Areas after completion.

## 9. Manager and Viewer language

Manager screens may show:

- current owner;
- active session;
- due-soon or overdue status;
- start/completion time;
- inspection readiness;
- unassigned/open exceptions;
- ownership changes by time window.

They should not imply that the system dictates every custodian's next physical stop. Manager assignment tools control ownership; employees retain practical order-of-work judgment inside that ownership unless directly instructed otherwise.

The public/read-only Viewer must not expose employee route details, device identity, internal notes, or manager controls.

## 10. UX redesign constraints

Habitat themes, improved motion, haptics, graphics, sound, bottom navigation, and shared components may improve the interface only if they preserve these rules.

Do not:

- put a decorative home screen between the employee and an NFC scan;
- require multiple taps before Start Cleaning after a valid scan;
- turn the assigned-area list into a mandatory route;
- use "next assignment" language for ordinary daily ownership;
- reorder employees across distant locations through UI logic;
- clear an active session during screen-off or temporary connection failure;
- ask an enrolled employee phone to select its employee name on every scan;
- remove the existing restroom/exhibit completion distinctions;
- weaken the timestamped NFC proof, issue reporting, offline queue, or manager inspection-readiness record.

## 11. Acceptance tests

Any employee app or redesign is not ready until it passes all of these on real kiosk hardware:

1. From the employee kiosk lock screen, tap a valid NFC tag and reach the correct location's Start Cleaning screen without opening a Scanner page.
2. Confirm the assigned employee identity is already fixed by the enrolled device.
3. Start and complete one restroom session through the real restroom completion form.
4. Start and complete one exhibit session through the real exhibit completion form.
5. Turn the screen off during an active timer; wake the phone and confirm the same timer/workflow remains.
6. Turn the screen off on an unfinished completion form; wake the phone and confirm all entries remain.
7. Disable connectivity, start/complete work, and verify the record queues locally and synchronizes later without duplicate submission.
8. Verify the assigned-area list shows all currently owned locations, with public restrooms visually first and no Current/Next sequence.
9. Verify a 9:45 ownership change updates only the relevant responsibility while an unchanged day remains one clean list.
10. Verify Tammy's example assignment does not produce a forced Teton → East End → Teton route.
11. Verify guest-issue and due/overdue messages target the current schedule-window owner.
12. Verify the manager dashboard receives the correct start, completion, employee, location, services, issues, and inspection-readiness record.

## 12. Relationship to the UX research plan

This document is the operational authority for employee field behavior. Where `docs/memphis-zoo-ux-research-and-design-plan.md` describes a **current assignment**, **next location**, or a primary **Scan & Clean** destination, read that language as superseded by this document.

The evidence-based visual and interaction improvements remain valid. They must be applied around the actual Memphis Zoo workflow rather than replacing it with a generic task-routing model.
